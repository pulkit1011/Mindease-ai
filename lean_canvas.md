## Customer segment detail
Initial customer: a first-to-third-year engineering student aged 17-22, comfortable with a laptop/phone, who feels exam or workload stress but has not used counselling. Early adopters are those already tracking habits or journaling.

## Assumptions to test with real users (with ethics approval and consent)
1. Students feel enough stress that they would use a self-check-in tool.
2. They would trust a chatbot for everyday stress topics if limits are explicit.
3. They will return weekly without push notifications.
4. A local-only (no login) approach is acceptable, or conversely they want sync across devices.
5. Colleges would sponsor or recommend such a tool.
6. Self-care exercises are used and considered useful.

## Validation metrics and example targets (to be set after discussion with supervisor; these are goals, not results)
| Metric | Example target for a 4-week pilot with 30 volunteers |
|---|---|
| Onboarding completion | ≥ 70% |
| At least one check-in | ≥ 60% |
| Check-ins per active user per week | ≥ 2 |
| 4-week repeat usage | ≥ 30% |
| Activity completion (≥ 1 activity) | ≥ 40% |
| Satisfaction (mean of 1-5 survey) | ≥ 3.5 |

## Problem-solution fit and validation plan
1. **Interviews (week 1-2):** 10-15 students and 2 counsellors; ask about current coping and barriers (no leading questions about diagnosis).
2. **Concept test (week 2):** show this demo; record comprehension and trust.
3. **Pilot (week 3-6):** 20-30 consenting volunteers, anonymous usage counts only, short satisfaction survey, no collection of notes or chat text.
4. **Decision rule:** continue only if repeat usage and helpfulness targets are plausibly met *and* no safety concerns emerge; otherwise change or stop.
5. Obtain institutional ethics/permission before involving real users. Involve a counsellor to review safety messages.

## Ethical commercialisation
- Never sell or share wellness data; no advertising built on emotional data.
- Never use manipulative engagement tricks (streak guilt, fake urgency) or encourage dependence.
- Keep crisis and support information free and visible at all times.
- Be transparent about AI use and costs; allow export and deletion.
- Prefer institutional/grant funding over models that profit from vulnerability; if premium features exist, make them non-essential.
- Comply with applicable law on personal data (for India, review the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 with legal advice before collecting data from users).

## Summary
Promising as a student project and a hypothesis to test, but *unproven as a business*. Next step: customer discovery with proper consent and supervision.
